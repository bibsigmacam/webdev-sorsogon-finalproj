About Sorsogon

Province Profile

Sorsogon at a Glance

Sorsogon is the southernmost province of Luzon, the largest island in the Philippines. Nestled at the tip of the Bicol Peninsula and bounded by the Pacific Ocean to the east, San Bernardino Strait to the south, and the Ticao Pass to the west, it is a land of unmatched natural beauty.

The province is home to 14 municipalities and 2 component cities, with a population of over 800,000 people known as Sorsoganons. The capital is Sorsogon City, the primary commercial and administrative center.

Origins

Roots of a Province

The name "Sorsogon" is derived from the Bikol word surusuog, meaning "a place often visited by strangers" — a fitting name for a land that has been a crossroads of peoples and cultures for centuries. Long before Spanish colonizers arrived, the area was inhabited by indigenous Aeta and Malay-descended Bisaya peoples.

Early settlements thrived along the rich coastal areas, sustained by fishing, trade, and agriculture. The province's strategic location at the southern tip of Luzon made it a natural waypoint for traders, explorers, and missionaries.

Timeline

Key Historical Events

Pre-16th Century

Indigenous Settlements

The region is home to Aeta (Agta) communities and Malay settlers who established coastal barangays. Trade with Chinese merchants and neighboring Visayan islands was already flourishing.

1569

Spanish Colonization Begins

Spanish conquistadors led by Miguel López de Legazpi began consolidating control over Bicol. Franciscan and Augustinian missionaries followed, establishing missions and the first Christian communities.

1636

Establishment as a Province

Sorsogon was formally established as a separate administrative province under Spanish colonial rule, carved out from the larger Camarines province.

1898

Philippine Revolution

Following the Philippine Revolution against Spain, Sorsogon became part of the newly proclaimed Philippine Republic under Emilio Aguinaldo. Local heroes played a significant role in the regional resistance.

1941–1945

World War II

Japanese forces occupied Sorsogon. The province saw intense guerrilla activity, and many towns sustained significant damage during the liberation campaign in 1945.

2000 & 2001

Modern Milestones

The capital town of Sorsogon was converted into a component city (RA 8806). In 2001, Donsol gained international fame when the WWF endorsed its whale shark tourism program as a global model for responsible wildlife tourism.

Location & Terrain

Where Land Meets Sea

Sorsogon occupies the southernmost tip of Luzon. It is bounded to the north by Albay Province, to the east by the Pacific Ocean, to the south by the San Bernardino Strait, and to the west by the Ticao Pass and Burias Pass.

The province covers approximately 2,140 square kilometers. Its terrain is varied — from volcanic mountains in the interior to extensive coastal plains and a deeply indented coastline stretching over 200 kilometers.

Sorsogon is home to the active Bulusan Volcano, which rises to 1,565 meters above sea level. The interior highlands are blanketed in dense tropical rainforest, harboring exceptional biodiversity.

Administration

Provincial Authority

Sorsogon is governed by an elected executive Governor and Vice-Governor, along with legislative board members of the Sangguniang Panlalawigan (Provincial Board). Central administrative operations are headquartered in the Provincial Capitol in Sorsogon City.

The province forms an integral component of Region V (Bicol Region) within national executive operational structures. Individual municipalities execute targeted local ordinances managing civil defense, infrastructure, and local parks management.

Congressional Representation

The province is split into two congressional districts: the 1st District administers northern and western coastal municipalities, while the 2nd District handles southern and eastern networks including the capital city zone. Each district elects a representative to the national Congress.

Livelihood

Economic Overview

Sorsogon's economy is anchored in agriculture, fishing, and a growing tourism sector. The province is primarily rural, with a large portion of the workforce engaged in rice and abaca farming, coconut production, and small-scale fishing along its extensive coastline.

Tourism — particularly eco-tourism centered on whale shark watching in Donsol — has become an increasingly important driver of income and employment.

The provincial government is also investing in renewable energy, given the province's volcanic and geothermal potential, and in developing special economic zones to attract industrial investment.

Learning & Development

Provincial Education Profile

Sorsogon has a well-developed education system including public and private institutions from elementary through tertiary levels. The province achieves a literacy rate of over 97% — among the highest in the entire Bicol region.

The Department of Education (DepEd) handles management across public elementary and secondary structures, fortified by local government unit scholarships and alternative technical training programs.
