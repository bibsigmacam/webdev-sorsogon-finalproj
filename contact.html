Getting Here & Around

Transportation Guide

Sorsogon is accessible from Manila and other major cities by land and sea. Within the province, a combination of buses, jeepneys, tricycles, and boats link all municipal destinations.

🚌
Long-Haul Bus

Air-conditioned overnight sleeper buses depart from Pasay/Cubao terminals in Manila. Travel time is approximately 12–14 hours direct to Sorsogon City.

✈️
By Air

Nearest airport: Bicol International Airport (LPA) in Daraga, Albay — approximately 75 km north of Sorsogon City. Take a van or bus from Legazpi.

🚗
Private Vehicle

Drive via the South Luzon Expressway (SLEX) connecting to the Maharlika Highway. Estimated 10–12 hours from Metro Manila depending on traffic.

⛴️
RORO Ferry

Matnog Port operates RORO ferries to Northern Samar — the national gateway connecting Luzon to the Visayas island chain.

Getting Around Within Sorsogon

🚐 Public Jeepneys & Vans

The main regional transit. Regular jeepney and express van routes depart from Sorsogon City's integrated terminal to all 14 municipalities including Donsol, Gubat, Matnog, and Bulusan.

🛺 Motorized Tricycles

Ideal for short door-to-door hops within town centers. Always agree on the fare before boarding. Standard rate is ₱10–15 per person for local trips.

⛵ Banca (Outrigger Boat)

Essential for reaching Subic Beach, Tikling Island, Paguriran Lagoon, and whale shark trips in Donsol. Charter rates are regulated by the local tourism office.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from travelers planning a visit to Sorsogon Province.

Yes. Donsol whale shark interactions are closely managed under strict eco-tourism guidelines developed with the WWF-Philippines. Only snorkeling is permitted — no scuba diving. A trained Butanding Interaction Officer (BIO) is assigned to every boat to ensure safe and responsible encounters. Touching, riding, or flash photography is strictly prohibited.

Sorsogon City serves as the central transport hub. Public jeepneys and express passenger vans depart regularly from the integrated city terminal to all towns including Gubat, Donsol, Matnog, and Bulusan. For short hops within any town center, motorized tricycles are widely available and affordable.

Yes, nominal environmental and maintenance fees apply at most eco-zones. Bulusan Lake charges approximately ₱50 per visitor, plus kayak rental fees. Subic Beach in Matnog requires a regulated banca charter from Matnog Port — rates are set by the local tourism office. Always carry cash as ATMs can be limited in remote areas.

The dry season from March to May is ideal for beaches and outdoor activities. For whale shark encounters, the peak season is February to April, though sightings occur November through June. The Kasanggayahan Festival in October is a great time to experience local culture. Avoid typhoon months (July–September) for water-based activities.

Trekking is permitted when PHIVOLCS (Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology) declares Alert Level 0. Always check the current advisory before visiting. When trekking is allowed, you must obtain permits from the Protected Area Office in Irosin and hire a registered local guide. The volcano is permanently monitored and local authorities respond quickly to any changes in activity.

The Philippines uses 220V AC at 60Hz. Standard plug types are flat two-pin (Type A) and three-pin (Type B). If your devices are rated for 110V, you will need a step-down voltage converter. Most hotels and lodges in Sorsogon City have standard outlets; remote eco-lodges may have limited power supply.

Yes, Sorsogon City has multiple ATMs from major Philippine banks (BDO, BPI, Landbank, Metrobank). Donsol and Gubat also have ATMs. However, remote municipalities like Matnog and Bulusan have limited or no ATM access — always carry sufficient cash before heading to island or mountain destinations.
